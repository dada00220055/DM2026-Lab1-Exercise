# Homework Report

After loading the dataset, I inspected the documents (`inspect_data_2`). I found that it was not always easy to tell which sentiment category a post belonged to by reading it. I also learned that duplicate posts could distort the DTM and feature-selection results by giving repeated text extra weight, so I removed duplicates (`check_duplicates_5`).

The [document-length distribution](../plots/陳穎達_homework_plot_describe_data_6.png) (`describe_data_6`) suggested that neutral posts tended to be shorter: their median and interquartile range were lower than those of the other categories. The term-frequency results (`term_frequency_8`) were still dominated by common stopwords, which are frequent but may not be useful for distinguishing sentiment.

When applying the variance filter (`variance_filter_11`), I considered how to choose the threshold. I initially tried 0.05, but lowered it to 0.01 after noticing potentially useful terms near the cutoff. I also compared Pearson and Spearman filtering (`pearson_filter_13` and `spearman_filter_14`). For example, “doing” was retained only by Spearman. This helped me review the difference between the methods: Pearson measures linear association using the original values, while Spearman measures association between ranks. Therefore, “doing” may appear more consistently and monotonically with relatively fewer extreme outliers.

For pattern mining (`mine_patterns_16` and `mine_patterns_17`), I chose a minimum support based on the category size and used MaxFPGrowth on category `1.0`, comparing TF-IDF and variance filtering. The TF-IDF results included colloquial and topical words, such as “good” and “market” that were absent from the variance-based results. These words may still be useful for interpreting the posts. The two filters use different criteria: variance filtering removes terms at the extremes of the variance distribution, whereas TF-IDF considers term frequency and document frequency.

The [PCA](../plots/陳穎達_homework_plot_reduce_pca_18.png) (`reduce_pca_18`) and [UMAP](../plots/陳穎達_homework_plot_reduce_umap_19.png) (`reduce_umap_19`) plots did not clearly separate the three sentiment categories. One possible reason is that posts with different sentiments share many words, which can dominate the DTM. The pattern-mining results also suggested that overlapping patterns offered limited information for distinguishing sentiment.
