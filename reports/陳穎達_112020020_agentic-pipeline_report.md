# Agentic Pipeline Report

I analyzed four categories from 20 Newsgroups: `comp.graphics`, `soc.religion.christian`, `sci.med`, and `alt.atheism`. The [document-length comparison](../plots/陳穎達_agentic-pipeline_plot_describe_data_5.png) (`describe_data_5`) shows that most articles in each category were under 5,000 characters, although every category had a few outliers exceeding 10,000 characters. I also built a document-term matrix (DTM). Its [heatmap](../plots/陳穎達_agentic-pipeline_plot_dtm_heatmap_9.png) (`dtm_heatmap_9`) shows that almost all entries in the displayed subset of 20 documents and 20 terms were zero.

I used `pearson_filter_tool` to examine terms associated with `sci.med` (`pearson_filter_11`). I recall seeing terms such as “medicine” and “treatment,” although many of the other terms did not appear as clearly related to the topic. The [feature correlation matrix](../plots/陳穎達_agentic-pipeline_plot_feature_correlation_12.png) (`feature_correlation_12`) was dominated by common words such as “the” and “of.” These stopwords remained in the DTM, and the matrix selected terms with high variance, so it was difficult to interpret correlations among topic-specific words.

During pattern mining, my computer ran out of memory and I had to restart it. I then sampled 200 documents and increased the minimum support relative to the number of documents in the category, which allowed me to find some patterns. 

Finally, I projected the DTM into two dimensions using PCA. In the [PCA plot](../plots/陳穎達_agentic-pipeline_plot_reduce_pca_6.png) (`reduce_pca_6`), the four categories overlap substantially near the center, while a few points lie far away; the first two components therefore do not clearly separate the categories. I also compared cosine similarities for documents from the same and different categories. My comparisons suggested that cosine similarity alone was not enough to determine whether a pair of documents belonged to the same category.
